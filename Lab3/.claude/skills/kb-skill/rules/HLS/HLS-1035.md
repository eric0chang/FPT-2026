- stp (stalled, the global default): no usage constraints and typically the fewest resources, but it freezes while input data is missing and cannot empty itself; in dataflow this can lead to more deadlocks, and results already computed may be stuck if inputs for later iterations never come.
- flp (flushing): stops taking input when the data-valid at its entry is low, yet keeps advancing the stages that still hold data until the final input reaches the output. Costs: II can be larger, and resource use grows from reduced sharing when II>1. With hls::tasks the tool selects flp automatically to avoid deadlock.
- frp (free-running, flushable): stp and flp stall through blocking signals wired to register enables; on deep pipelines handling wide data inside a DATAFLOW region these become high-fanout nets, a leading cause of timing-closure failures that logic synthesis and place-and-route cannot repair. frp removes those enable connections and lowers fanout and wire length without relying on flop cloning. Costs: FIFO buffers on the blocking output ports (moderate resource increase), mux delay at those ports, a possible performance hit from early validation of forward-pressure triggers, and higher power because registers are clocked even without data.
- Automatic choice: flp with hls::tasks; frp if pipeline control needs high fanout and the frp requirements are met; otherwise stp.
- `style=` is only a hint: a loop that does not qualify for the requested style falls back to stp. The per-pragma style leaves the global default (syn.compile.pipeline_style / config_compile -pipeline_style, stp unless changed) untouched, e.g. `#pragma HLS pipeline II=1 style=flp`.
Also covered in UG1399 Ch.16 HLS Pragmas > pragma HLS pipeline, p. 540-542.

Source: UG1399 v2023.2, Ch.3 Loops Primer > Flushing Pipelines and Pipeline Types, pp. 66-68.
