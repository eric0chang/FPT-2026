- Operators: `facc` (accumulate), `fmacc` (multiply-accumulate), `fmadd` (multiply-add), enabled with `syn.op` lines; the manual's example is `syn.op=op:fmacc precision:high`.
- Standard: a true floating-point accumulator, fine for most uses; II=1 on Versal, on other devices an II typically of 3-5 depending on clock and device.
- Low: integer accumulator with pre-/post-scalers (60-bit for single, 100-bit for double inputs); a plain `sum += a[i]*b[i]` loop then pipelines at II=1 with no source change, at about 3X the resources of standard.
- High: one fused multiply-add rounded once at the end, with one extra precision bit; uses more resources than the unfused form.
- Cosim vs C++ simulation can mismatch: low has too little precision, high has more than the C++ model.
- Manual inconsistency: low precision is said to be non-Versal only, yet a later sentence claims its II=1 accumulation works on all devices; only the non-Versal case is unambiguous.

Source: UG1399 v2023.2, Ch.6 Data Types > Floating-Point Accumulator and MAC, pp. 107-108.
