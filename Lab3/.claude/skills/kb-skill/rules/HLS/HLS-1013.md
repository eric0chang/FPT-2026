- In the manual's diamond example A fills two different arrays, one read by B and one by C; D consumes the outputs of both.
- B and C then execute concurrently after A, and D waits for both.
- The manual makes this conditional on B and C not needing to share a computation resource.
- Successive invocations still run one after another; overlapping them needs double buffering (task-level pipelining).

Source: UG1399 v2023.2, Ch.1 Design Principles > Combining the Three Paradigms, pp. 34-35.
