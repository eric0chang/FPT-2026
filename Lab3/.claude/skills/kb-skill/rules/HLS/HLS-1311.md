- Using hls::task in the top function makes the top a parallel context. Sequential callers reach tasks through a dataflow function, whose regular processes produce and consume the task streams.
- Under ap_ctrl_none regions: nothing above it may be a sequential FSM or a dataflow region with a different protocol, not even one created by accident; the start/ready/done/continue handshakes then vanish up to the top.
- In a dataflow region: its input streams come from non-ap_ctrl_none processes or regions called earlier, and its output streams go to ones called later. Those predecessors supply the enclosing region's ap_start/ap_ready handshake and the successors its ap_done/ap_continue handshake.
- Allowed nesting: tasks at the top, inside another task network, or between regular processes in a dataflow region; control-driven dataflow at the top, inside another dataflow region, or inside a sequential region.
- To nest control-driven dataflow under a task, put it in a sequential function that the task body runs. Sequential regions and pipelines likewise sit inside a task's body, not directly in a control- or data-driven region.
- Note: the nesting list also says data-driven cannot be directly inside control-driven, yet tasks inside a dataflow region are explicitly allowed (p.751, p.756); the dataflow-region placement is what the examples use.
Also covered in UG1399 Ch.8 Interfaces of the HLS Design > Execution Modes of HLS Designs > Block-Level Control Protocols > ap_ctrl_none, pp. 232-233.
