- Omitting `type=` therefore fully decomposes the array: individual registers (partition) or one register of N x M bits for N elements of M bits (reshape).
- block = `factor` sub-arrays of consecutive elements; cyclic = `factor` sub-arrays with elements dealt round-robin. With factor=2 on N elements: block gives {0..N/2-1} and {N/2..N-1}; cyclic gives the even and the odd indices. For reshape, factor k divides the depth by k and multiplies the word width by k.
- factor need not divide the size; the last sub-array then holds fewer elements: block partition of 13 elements by 4 gives sub-arrays of 3, 3, 3 and 4; block reshape of 17 8-bit elements by 4 gives five 32-bit words, the last holding one element in its low byte and otherwise empty.
- dim examples: for `int m[10][6][4]`, dim=3 gives 4 arrays of `[10][6]`, dim=1 gives 10 arrays of `[6][4]`, dim=0 gives 240 individual registers. `type=block factor=2 dim=2` on a [6][4] array: partition yields arrays of [6][2]; reshape yields a [6][2] array with doubled word width. `type=complete dim=0` turns an 8-bit [4][2][2] array into one 128-bit register.
- ARRAY_RESHAPE also accepts off=true to switch reshaping off for one variable.
- Vitis HLS may also completely partition arrays by itself; the element-count threshold for that is `syn.array_partition.complete_threshold`.
Also covered in UG1399 Ch.4 Arrays Primer > Array Accesses and Performance > Array Partitioning, pp. 86-88.
