- Why: entering and leaving an inner loop each costs one clock cycle in RTL; a flattened nest is one loop without those transitions and can be optimized as a whole.
- Cost of an unflattened nest: one cycle to enter and one to leave the rolled inner loop on every outer iteration (100 outer iterations -> 200 extra cycles).
- Imperfect nest (variable inner bound, or code outside the inner loop): HLS cannot flatten it; rewrite the code, or unroll the loops nested inside the body, so the nest becomes perfect.
- The pragma flattens its loop together with the (semi-)perfect loops above it; `loop_flatten off` keeps one particular loop unflattened.
- HLS also flattens automatically where possible: pipelining the inner loop of a 20×20 nest produced one loop reported as LOOP_I_LOOP_J with 400 iterations. If the log or synthesis report still lists the loops separately, the nest was not flattened and extra enter/exit cycles remain.
Also covered in UG1399 Ch.3 Loops Primer > Working with Nested Loops, p. 77-79.

Source: UG1399 v2023.2, Ch.16 HLS Pragmas > pragma HLS loop_flatten, pp. 531-533.
