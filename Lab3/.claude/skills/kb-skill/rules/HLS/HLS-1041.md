Example: two 4-iteration loops at 1 cycle/iteration take 1 (enter) + 4 + 1 (switch) + 4 + 1 (exit) = 11 cycles; merged, 6.
- Merging removes the cycles spent switching between separate loop bodies, can let the bodies run in parallel, and opens sharing and logic optimization.
- Placement: LOOP_MERGE attempts to merge every loop in the scope where it is placed. In a function body it merges all its consecutive loops; to merge a loop with its sibling loops, put the pragma in the enclosing function or region, not inside that loop.
- The transform has limits and can fail; refactoring manually (e.g. one loop with an if/else instead of two loops with complementary conditions) gives the same result.
- In the manual's example, a dual-port block RAM is what lets the merged add and subtract proceed in parallel.
Also covered in UG1399 Ch.16 HLS Pragmas > pragma HLS loop_merge, pp. 533-534.

Source: UG1399 v2023.2, Ch.3 Loops Primer > Merging Loops, pp. 75-76.
